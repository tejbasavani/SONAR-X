A transmitter that reads the water before it speaks

A low-power, real-time software-defined sonar transmitter payload for AUVs — reshaping its own chirp, sweep, and pulse-coded output as depth, turbidity, and range change beneath it.

STM32G4 100–500 kHz LFM / Sweep / PCP DMA + hardware timers
DEPTH 0400m BRG 214°

Overview

01

Traditional sonar transmitters fire a fixed waveform regardless of conditions — a high-frequency chirp scatters instantly in murky water, and a low-frequency one blurs the image it returns. Underwater conditions change constantly with depth, turbidity, temperature, and salinity, so a fixed transmitter is always a compromise somewhere along an AUV's route.

This payload behaves instead like a software-defined radio for sound: it senses the environment, decides on the right waveform for it, and synthesizes that waveform in real time — without ever stalling the processor or draining the battery computing trigonometry on the fly.

Senses first

Reads depth, turbidity, and range through the ADC before every adaptation cycle.

Adapts on the fly

Switches between LFM chirps, geometric sweeps, and phase-coded pulses without pausing transmission.

Never stalls the CPU

DMA and hardware timers carry every sample — the processor is free the moment a pulse starts streaming.

05

The analog front-end and mixed-signal interface are built to withstand the rigorous demands of AUV payloads. The board strictly partitions high-speed digital logic from sensitive analog acoustic channels.

Top Layer (F.Cu)

Signal Routing
  • Zoned Placement: Digital core (STM32G4) isolated from the high-power analog output (TPA3116).
  • High-Current Paths: Thick trace routing from the 7-12V entry through the AMS1117 regulator to the transducer block.
  • Signal Integrity: Point-to-point SPI routing to the MCP4922 to eliminate crosstalk.
